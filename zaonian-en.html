Experience & Works

Born September 11, 1997. Ancestral home: Chifeng, Inner Mongolia. Operates under multiple independent aliases — Alive, JonahGillen, davinci, Weifeng, among others — and undertakes commissions under the name "Yorozuya". His real name is occasionally cited as Wang. No public photograph is available.

"It's not that I don't love myself. I just can't do anything with myself."
"I left my shadow in yesterday. Today I went out and forgot to bring my body."
"Some spend a lifetime learning to say goodbye. I was born knowing how."

Early Years: Chifeng

He is believed to have spent his youth in Chifeng. At twelve, an essay titled "What Lies at the End of the Grassland" was later mentioned in a now-inaccessible online post about "post-90s cultural memory in Chifeng." Boxing training is also believed to have begun in his teens; in underground matches around Chifeng and Tongliao, he became known for swift evasion and calm counterattacks. Sina Sports' regional channel noted in a 2012 roundup that "a competitor at a Chifeng event sparked discussion with unconventional tactics," without further details.

"Before the fist landed on me, the wind took the blow for me first."
"Only after the ring lights went out did I feel myself glow."

Adult Years: Across the Provinces

In the years after reaching adulthood, he moved between Shandong and Guangdong. Scattered accounts refer to an unnamed young man who intervened in disputes and then left without further contact, though specifics remain unclear. Around 2017, he settled in Beijing's Fangzhuang district. He never attended any art academy. His early livelihood was a mosaic: by day, a traffic-signal debugger; at dusk, a reseller of second-hand art and goods at Panjiayuan; by night, a poet and clandestine performer. According to several longtime stallholders at Panjiayuan, a "young man surnamed Wang" repeatedly intervened on behalf of bullied vendors, striking fast and leaving without staying for meals.

Around 2016, Science and Technology Daily's digital platform reported on vulnerabilities in urban intelligent traffic systems, noting that several intersections in an unnamed city exhibited abnormal signal phases late at night for about two weeks. Sohu Tech republished the piece; several comments speculated it might have been "the work of an insider."

Shortly after, he entered the security field in a non-staff technical role. In 2017, Phoenix Culture published a review of the "anonymous creator phenomenon in the independent art scene," describing an individual with "the multiple identities of artist, critic, and businessperson who never appears in public." That same year, The Paper's "Ideas" column published a special report on "Art and Surveillance," anonymously quoting an artist formerly employed in security on themes of urban control and bodily discipline — a close match.

His "Yorozuya" commission practice began during this period. In 2018, Tencent News' "Guyu Lab" published a feature quoting a source referred to as "Mr. W" who said, "I take any job, never ask who they are, and never leave my own name." Sina "Weitainxia" later included "Yorozuya" in a roundup of online anonymous culture, calling it "a microcosm of the internet's ghost economy."

"I taught the traffic light to be silent, and it taught me to wait."
"I know every red light in the city, but not one knows me."
"I've walked through crowds for years, and still not a single person has called my name."

2017–2020: Creative Period

From 2017, under the pen name "Weifeng," he published art criticism in independent media. That same year he completed his first public performance The Back of the Plain. In 2018 he completed Signal Cycle and Underground: Garage Series. In late 2018, NetEase News' "Kan Ke" column published a photo essay titled Performers in the City's Gaps, featuring a blurred nighttime photo of a figure at an intersection captioned simply "Anonymous performance artist, taken at a Beijing intersection."

In 2019 he completed Night Bus, Pastoral Walk, and Silent Round. In 2020 he completed Names on the Wall and Portraits of the Anonymous. In late 2020, a local news item on Jinri Toutiao reported "large quantities of chalk-written names found on the wall of a demolition market in Fengtai District," deleted within 24 hours but preserved in screenshots. That same week, Beijing Evening News ran a piece titled "Mysterious Signatures on a Demolition Wall."

By conservative estimate, this period produced over a dozen performance works, around sixty paper works, and numerous digital files, none signed with a consistent name.

"I wrote down so many names, just wanting to see which one would be remembered. In the end, not a single one."
"Every name was me. Every name wasn't."

Post-2021: Overseas Sojourn

In 2021, under "davinci," he mounted a solo exhibition The Poetics of Control in East London. The Guardian's arts section mentioned the exhibition in a single sentence within a 2021 piece, calling it "a show by a nameless artist with no audience." European Times' UK edition also referenced the exhibition without naming the artist. Sohu Art News republished the feature under the headline "Names That Disappeared in Britain's Underground Art Circles."

That same year, under "Alive," he performed twice at an underground venue in Tokyo's Kōenji district. Mainichi Shimbun's culture website "BOOKSTAND" published a short article titled The Ghost of Kōenji, describing a performance with "no promotion, no introduction of performers, vanished as soon as it ended." Global Times' culture section mentioned "a Chinese artist using an English name" who disappeared after performing in Kōenji.

"Beijing has no grasslands, only fenced-in vastness."
"I'm not afraid of getting lost. I'm afraid that every road leads somewhere I don't want to return."

2021–2023: Disappearance

From the second half of 2021 through early 2023, he vanished entirely from all public channels. Encrypted poetry publications ceased; all Yorozuya commissions were suspended; his art criticism stopped. For approximately a year and a half, no verifiable activity was recorded. In early 2023, Phoenix News published an investigative report on the infiltration of transnational criminal networks in Southeast Asia, noting that a Chinese national operating under an assumed identity had spent an extended period in northern Myanmar transmitting intelligence before being covertly extracted. Around the same period, CCTV's "Legal Report" aired a segment citing an unnamed undercover operative who had provided key leads. The timeline of the disappearance overlaps with these reports.

"I learned silence in a place where nobody tells the truth, only to discover that silence itself is the biggest flaw."
"In that place, those who told the truth were treated as madmen. Those who stayed silent were treated as spies."
"During the years I disappeared, no one asked where I'd gone. When I returned, no one asked where I'd come from."

2023–Present: Ghost Operations and Ongoing Practice

In early 2023, he reappeared online, resuming anonymous art sales and Yorozuya commissions. In 2023, a popular post on a Chinese social platform titled "I bought a painting, don't know who made it, but I always feel it's staring at me" featured a dark city map. Sanlian Life Weekly's digital platform ran a discussion on anonymous art trading, quoting a buyer: "I paid the price of a meal for a painting. The artist refused to sign it. He said that once a piece is signed, it becomes a commodity, no longer a work."

Simultaneously, under "Yorozuya," he continues to take on unconventional commissions. In 2024, CCTV.com's culture channel quoted a line attributed to "an anonymous artist" in an article on anonymous creation in contemporary art.

"I've survived by selling my own shadow — but there's no sun today."
"I sold all my tomorrows and got back a pile of yesterdays."
"I don't sign, not because I'm afraid of being discovered. It's because once signed, the work no longer belongs to the one looking at it."

2022–2026: Selected Works

  • Darkroom Drawings (2022–2023): Paper works. Ultra-fine silver lines on black grounds tracing city maps, approximately twenty pieces.
  • Encrypted Poetry Manuscripts (2023–2024): Digital files. Short poems paired with noise images, issued as unique editions.
  • Anonymous Letters (2023–2025): Text works. One reads: "I am writing to you because I don't know you. If you receive this, please burn it, and go on with your life." In 2024, a recipient posted the full letter on Douban, sparking widespread discussion before deleting it.
  • Yorozuya Archive (2024–2026): Comprehensive project. Commission records compiled into an anonymous PDF.
  • Signal and Interval video (2025): Reportedly a re-edited video from the Japan performances.
  • Border Notes (2026): Mixed text and image work. Said to be his most significant recent work, never publicly released; according to those close to him, its content relates to the period of his disappearance.
"I sold myself for two hundred bucks. Bought a pack of cigarettes, still had one hundred eighty left."
"Loneliness isn't having no one to talk to. It's everyone talking, but none of it needs your reply."

Identity Network

He maintains multiple identities, none overlapping: a silent amateur in boxing circles; a trader surnamed Wang at the flea market; a user who appears and vanishes on poetry forums; a performer in the independent art scene who refuses to be photographed; a critic online who never reveals his name; a non-staff worker in the security field with no personnel file; during the period of his disappearance, a nameless figure on a mission. All share a single core: a refusal to be indexed.

When an independent art institution once sought to include his materials in an archive, he declined. No verifiable photograph has ever circulated publicly. His four known aliases — Alive, JonahGillen, davinci, and Weifeng — each correspond to different facets of his practice. The actual number of pseudonyms used is said to be far greater.

"A ghost doesn't need a name, but needs an archive. Otherwise, it's truly dead."
"Being remembered is dangerous. Being forgotten, too. The safest thing is to make it impossible to tell whether you really exist, or whether you were only imagined."
