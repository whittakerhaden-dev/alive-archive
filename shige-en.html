Poetry Fragments

The following pieces are believed to originate from manuscript drafts and encrypted publishing records spanning various periods. Attribution has been confirmed through cross-referencing of multiple sources. Some exist only as fragments; the original complete texts are no longer retrievable. Arranged in order of collation, not strictly by date of composition. Over one hundred twenty pieces, with further collation ongoing.
Five in the Morning
Woke at 5 a.m., birds already calling outside the window. They don't take weekends off.
Magic Trick
A grand magic show, the moment of miracle. Countless Chinese people, swarming, suddenly transformed — into American citizens, just like that.
Supplies
Just went to the supermarket to buy vegetables. So many supplies in there. Suddenly I realized I'd started looking at everything before me through the eyes of "supplies."
Golden Age
The previous second, to the next second, is the golden age.
Disaster
Disaster wears different masks and favors more and more people.
The Button
Last night I clearly pressed the button to destroy the world. This morning I still woke from a dream as usual. What a pity.
Motorcycle Diary
Rode the motorcycle to breakfast, rode it to play ball, rode it home.
Three Wild Horses
My motorcycle is a wild horse
I am a wild horse
Wang Yingru is a wild horse

A wild horse and a wild horse
riding a wild horse
piercing the black depression of the city
despair and sorrow

In the deathly silent night
galloping
Poplar Fluff
The sun is beautiful today. The air is full of poplar fluff. It tastes good. Try it yourself — just open your mouth and you'll catch some.
Dream Fragment
Got up to pee, interrupting the dream I was having. All I remember is the feeling, childlike, of needing to go home to find my mother.
Footprints in Snow
After kindergarten, father and son walk home through the snow. The son says: Dad, do you hear that sound? I've made the snow cry from being stepped on.
Time Machine
The pandemic is a time machine. How many people, in an instant, have returned to the Qin Dynasty.
Wolf
The wolf is coming, the wolf is coming. When the wolf doesn't come, the heart is uneasy. The wolf comes. We are eaten.
Winter Sun
Looking at the flags in the distance, the wind seems to blow from the north, and certainly continues southward. But I feel it also comes from the west.

I walk west, unhurried. My ears and face are ceaselessly blown by the wind, to the point of achieving a sense of stillness. It is feeling itself that has stilled.
Almost a Kind of Sadness
I dreamed of the end of the world. People who love each other will part and die no matter what. As if moving on to a new life, but no longer on the same path.

And someone told me the dead will return from hell. It sounds frightening. But perhaps like in a film, all the dead characters stand up at the end, dust themselves off, and shake hands.
Peanuts
The soul is the flesh
The flesh is rice wine
Rice wine is peanuts
Peanuts are peanuts
Red Wine
Open the red wine
One sip for you
One sip for me
You give me a sip
I give you a sip

I love you
You love me
I give you money
You call it prostitution money
The Insomniac's Clock
1:35 / 2:01 / 2:57 / 3:46 / 4:23 / 4:58 / 5:39 / 6:14

The insomniac is a laborer of the dark hours, building a lighthouse upon the reef of time.
Subway
The subway races on, passengers without end stretching beyond sight. These faces with features erased, huge heads, stuffed with bloated desires and boundless thoughts. One by one, they detach from bodies, rise in sequence, then burst high in the air, brilliant as flowers.
Quarrel
A chicken talks to a duck, a duck talks to a goose, a goose talks to a bird, a bird talks to a plane, a plane talks to a phoenix, a phoenix talks to a peacock, a peacock talks to an ostrich, the ostrich buries its head in the sand and talks to itself.
Link
I put my hand into your mouth, you put your foot into my pants, we exchange the secrets of this world.
Praise
Let politics return to politics, let ideologies return to ideologies, let rhetoric return to rhetoric. Tonight, certain words must be washed clean before we can speak of the world again. Tonight, woman must be returned to woman before she can be praised.
Winter Notes
Outside the window below zero. My body temperature thirty-six five, my heart eighty-five. My northern lover is sleeping. The southern hedgehog has shot all its arrows. A friend sent me a dark green wine glass. At dawn, across the distance, we drank Keats's poems, Bach's fugues, and some fresh melancholy.
Qingming Guide
If you feel lonely late at night, go out into the street, randomly grab a few ghosts and bring them home. With youth as your companion, sit close and talk through the night, until your tongues are covered in moss.
Festival
My friend Henry Miller once told me that kangaroos have two penises, one for everyday use, one reserved for festivals. This vividly explains the meaning of the Chinese word "festival" — a day of restraint. How exquisite.
Moments
He posts photos of his girlfriend on social media almost every day, three hundred sixty-five days a year, relentless and unwavering. Which means he has three girlfriends: one living alone, one living in a world of two, one living in his Moments feed.
Hamster
When spring comes, where is the hamster.
Airfield
A plane with its lights on moves slowly across the night sky over Songzhuang. The music from the square is overwhelming. People are happy. Collective happiness surpasses the happiness of one alone.
Studio
This fly lands on the rim of my water cup and crawls back and forth, several times now. I shout and push out my palm, and it falls into the water.
Flight
I fly, often because of hunger.
Disassembly
I am a disassembled machine, parts scattered across the rooms of time and space. Please destroy my will once more, let the flesh leave the fingers that smoke.
Spring
All the brightness of spring is connected to summer.
Touch
My hands have touched filthy things. Now, I can only caress you with the back of my hand.
Chang'an
In Chang'an, sleep does not come.
Summer in Songzhuang
In Songzhuang, we drank until dawn, we spoke incoherently, we picked up the wheat stalks at the door, we rubbed off the husks, and swallowed the grains.
Market
Those wide flat beans and red beans I've never eaten, I'll eat them all. That misnamed spiny fish, I'll eat it too. I'll confiscate the empty bottle and leftover food from the homeless man dozing against the roadside at noon. And that strange woman, I must take her to find that place, that mysterious southeastern direction she points to with her finger.
Beisi Village
Holding an umbrella, lost under the sun.
Passerby
I lean against the wall outside the supermarket, waiting for someone who may or may not still come. The rain stops and starts, starts and stops.
Quitting Smoking
The cigarettes are finished, the butt must be stubbed out, like snuffing out an unnecessary thought.
Returning Home
Yesterday I sowed sunflower seeds. Today a fine rain falls. In less than a week the seeds will sprout. In autumn, come here and eat sunflower seeds.
Hat
The sweater is light blue, the palm is pink, the stock market is green, the train is also green. Only when the hat is green is it truly green.
Working Out
Lift weights, sleep, walk, sleep. The walkers are zombies, those on the gym equipment are puppets, the security guard is a marble sculpture, the square dancers are plump and gaudy fairies. And I am a wandering ghost.
Disorientation
The insomniac builds a lighthouse upon the reef of time. Each waking moment is an island. The sea is black, the waves are silent.
Sunflower
I planted a sunflower. It turns toward the sun every day. I sit beside it. It never turns toward me.
Power
Power is a chair. The one who sits on it thinks he has grown taller, but it's only the chair that is high. When the chair is taken away, he returns to his original height.
Class
I look down from my twentieth-floor balcony. The rich look like ants, the poor also look like ants. But the ants don't know I'm watching them.
Love
Love is a rubber band. Pull too tight and it snaps; leave it too loose and it gets taken away.
Worldliness
People spend a lifetime learning how to become someone else, then spend their final years regretting not becoming themselves.
Tombstone
The name on a tombstone is for the living. The dead don't need names.
Election
They ask you to choose between red and blue, as if you really had a choice. In truth, you can only choose whether to dream awake or dream asleep.
Salary
A monthly salary is a rope, one end tied to you, the other to your desires. The farther you walk, the tighter the rope pulls.
Moments 2
I posted a landscape photo on Moments. Fifty-six people liked it. I stood downstairs for ten minutes. Not a single person passed by.
Truth
Truth is a shard of broken glass. Each person picks up a piece and says this is the whole.
Faith
They erect a statue of a god in the middle of the square, then do business around it.
Education
School teaches you how to answer questions, but never how to question the questions themselves.
Hospital
A hospital is the fairest place. Rich and poor queue in the same line, waiting for the same bed. The only difference is, some people get one, some never do.
Prison
There are two kinds of prison: one with iron bars, one without. The one without, we call it an office.
News
News is today's breakfast. It goes stale by tomorrow. But you still have to eat it every day.
Marriage
Marriage is a joint bankruptcy liquidation between two people. Assets are merged, debts are split equally.
Divorce
Divorce is two people searching through the ruins for whatever they can still take away.
Child
A child is a blank sheet of paper. Adults fill it with all their own regrets.
Old Age
After getting old, time slows down, but the body speeds up — I mean, it breaks down faster.
Funeral
A funeral is the last social event held for the living. The main character has no speaking part.
Loneliness
Loneliness isn't eating alone. It's eating with two other people who are talking about someone you don't know.
Friend
A friend is your bank account balance. When the balance is high, friends are many. When it drops to zero, so do the friends.
Enemy
An enemy is more honest than a friend. At least he knows why he hates you.
Silence
Silence is a knife. It hurts you when you hold it; it hurts others when you throw it.
Language
Language is a bridge, but most people stand on either end shouting, never stepping onto the bridge itself.
History
History is the diary written by the victors. The defeated have no pen.
War
War is a game for politicians, a gamble for soldiers, and a bill for civilians.
Peace
Peace is the intermission between two wars.
Money
Money is a mirror, reflecting the true face of every person.
Poverty
Poverty is not the absence of money, but the absence of choice.
Wealth
Wealth is not having a lot of money, but having a lot of things you can lose.
Freedom
Freedom is a bird inside a cage, thinking it can fly to any corner of the cage.
Time
Time is the best doctor and the worst makeup artist.
Memory
Memory is a museum edited by oneself. All the exhibits are forgeries.
Forgetting
Forgetting is the best gift God gave to humanity. Pity we can never remember who sent it.
Birth
Birth is a forced journey. No one asked if you wanted to set out.
Death
Death is the cancellation of all debts, the termination of all questions.
Subway 2
On the subway, everyone stares at their phone, like a flock of sheep grazing with heads bowed. Occasionally someone looks up, and becomes an outcast.
Delivery
The delivery driver's electric scooter is faster than my dreams, but slower than my salary.
Rent
Rent is a monthly parasite that comes to collect. Once it has eaten all the host's dreams, it moves on.
Health Check
A health check is a bill telling you how much longer you can hold on.
Annual Party
The annual party is a sleeping pill the company feeds its employees, mixed with alcohol and raffle tickets.
Promotion
A promotion turns a sheep into a sheepdog, then tells it that it's closer to the wolves now.
Resignation
Resignation is untying your own rope, then discovering that outside is still a net.
City
A city is a vast machine. People are its parts. When they break, they are replaced. No one asks the parts how they feel.
Village
The village is the city's dumping ground, sheltering those who have been discarded.
ID Card
A card that proves who you are, when you yourself don't know who you are.
Password
We set countless passwords to protect our secrets, only to discover that the things we most want to hide, we dare not write down at all.
Mirror
Is the person in the mirror really me? He is more honest than I am. At least he does not smile.
Night
Night is the reverse side of day. So are people.
Morning
Morning is an expired cup of coffee. If you don't drink it, you'll be tired. If you do, it's bitter.
Fired
The day I was fired, the sun was beautiful. I stood outside the office for a while. The doorman had changed. He didn't recognize me.
Rental
The woman next door cried every night. I knocked once. She opened the door and smiled, said it was nothing. Later she moved out. A couple moved in. Every night they laughed.
Supermarket
The supermarket closes at nine-thirty. I went in at nine-twenty. The clerk looked at me. I looked at him. We both waited for the other to say something.
Stairwell
Someone smoked in the stairwell. Cigarette butts littered the floor. I lit one too, joined them. We didn't speak, just flicked ash into the same corner.
Bus
An old man stared at me the whole bus ride. When I got off, he suddenly said: You look like me when I was young. I said: And now? He didn't answer.
Fish Stall
The fishmonger's hands were red from cold. I asked if she was cold. She said yes, but the fish are colder. I bought one. While she scraped the scales, the fish still moved.
Print Shop
The print shop owner asked how many copies. I said one. He paused, said one copy is still a copy. I said yes, one is still one.
Rainy Night
It rained. I stood under a convenience store awning to wait it out. A delivery driver also took shelter. His phone rang. It was a late order. He looked at it, then rushed into the rain.
Hospital Corridor
The hospital corridor was full of people, all waiting. Waiting for their number, waiting for results, waiting to be discharged, waiting to die. I sat down and joined them.
Four in the Morning
At four in the morning, someone was sweeping the street outside my window. The broom against the ground was regular, like an ancient timepiece.
Train
On the train, a woman sat across from me. She ate two instant noodles, then started crying. I put a tissue on the table. She didn't take it. Later she got off. I put the tissue back.
Elevator
In the elevator, just me and a stranger. He pressed five, I pressed seven. We reached five, he didn't get off. We reached seven, I got off. As the doors closed, I saw he was still standing inside.
Rooftop
I went up to the rooftop once, to see the city. The wind was strong, blowing cigarette butts around. I stood there a long time, until someone came up to hang their laundry.
Courier
The courier called, said the package had arrived. I told him to leave it at the door. He said it needed a signature. I said you sign it. He said that's not allowed. I went down, he handed me a pen. I signed the neighbor's name.
Barbershop
The barber asked how much to cut. I said cut it short. He asked again how much. I said cut it until no one recognizes me. He paused, then started cutting. In the end he said: You're still recognizable.
Breakfast Shop
The breakfast shop owner remembered my order. Every time I went in, she said it first: one bowl of soy milk, two fried dough sticks. Later her shop closed. I never went back to that street.
Blackout
The power went out. I found a candle in the drawer. When I lit it, the room changed. So many shadows on the walls.
Trash Bin
A stray cat lived near the trash bin downstairs. Every night I brought it something to eat. One day it disappeared. The bin was still there. I kept passing by.
Traffic Light
Every time I wait at a red light, I count how many people are still on the other side. When it turns green, they walk over, I walk over. We pass each other, none of us knowing who the other is.
Empty Room
When I moved out, I left the key on the table. The room was empty, emptier than when I arrived. I closed the door and heard the echo.
